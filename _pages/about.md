I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at MIT, working with <a href="https://evlab.mit.edu/">Evelina Fedorenko</a>. My research asks how the brain processes language across the world's remarkable linguistic diversity — in both its languages and its speakers. On the language side, I study typologically diverse languages, from well-studied Indo-European languages to under-represented indigenous languages. On the speaker side, I study bilinguals, multilinguals, polyglots, and heritage speakers. Using neuroimaging methods combined with cross-cultural fieldwork, I ask what is universal about language processing in the brain, what varies across languages and speakers, and how the different networks in our brains are able to support this striking diversity.

I completed my PhD in <a href="https://shbtprogram.harvard.edu/">Speech and Hearing Bioscience and Technology</a> at Harvard University, working with <a href="https://evlab.mit.edu/">Evelina Fedorenko</a> and <a href="http://tedlab.mit.edu/">Edward Gibson</a>. Before that, I did a Master's in the Cognitive Neuroscience of Language at the <a href="https://www.bcbl.eu/">Basque Center on Cognition, Brain and Language</a> in San Sebastián, Spain, and a B.Sc. in Psychology at the <a href="https://www.uab.cat/">Autonomous University of Barcelona</a>.
